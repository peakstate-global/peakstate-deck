What I would change, and why

Claude Design could not run. Its MCP server is configured but rejected the login: FIRST_PARTY_AUTH_REJECTED, HTTP 403, with the hint to run /design-login. The /design slash command is still the consent-only form. So this is /impeccable alone, in the product register.

Two diagnoses, and the second is the bigger one.

The bar is built from emoji. Ten controls, every glyph an emoji or dingbat: 💬 👁 ◆ 📝 ☆ 🚫 ◇ ◼ ▶ 📋. Emoji render differently on every platform, carry colour nobody chose, and sit at whatever weight the font decides. The repository's own PRODUCT.md lists "emoji as iconography" as an anti-reference. This is the single change that does the most.

The overview teaches with a paragraph. Three lines of prose in the header explain clicking, dragging, S, H, C, Tab and Esc. Nobody reads instructions above a grid of pictures. And every tile carries four buttons at all times, so an eight-slide deck shows thirty-two identical buttons and none of them is the one you want.

The control bar

As it ships

now · 10 controls · about 800px wide at 1440

Proposed

4 / 22
proposed · same 10 controls · about 470px · rests at 62% until hovered

Five changes, each with a reason.

  • Inline SVG at one stroke weight, no emoji. Every glyph the same 1.5px stroke in currentColor, so hover, focus and disabled work on the icon as well as the button.
  • Three zones separated by 22px of nothing, not a hairline. The eye groups by proximity long before it notices a 1px rule. Zones are: this slide, the deck, the round trip.
  • Words only where the action leaves the slide: Overview, Present, Copy. Everything else is icon plus tooltip plus aria-label. That is a rule the eye can learn, where the current mix is not.
  • The slide position moves into the bar, replacing the runtime's separate footer counter and telling you at a glance which slide the state controls are acting on.
  • It rests at 62% opacity and wakes on hover or keyboard focus. Chrome that floats over content should not compete with it while you read.

  • Buttons appear on hover or keyboard focus, on that tile only. Three visible buttons instead of thirty-two, and the one you want is under the pointer already.
  • A status strip replaces four permanent buttons. Glyphs appear only when the state is true, so a plain slide is a plain tile and the eye finds the marked ones instantly.
  • The tile stops being a card. Today a bordered card contains a bordered thumbnail: a box inside a box. The thumbnail is the tile; the caption sits on the page.
  • The instructions collapse into a ? Keys button. The affordances teach themselves on hover; the key map is there for the person who wants it.
  • The toggles get one grammar. "Show starred only", "Show hidden slides", "Show branding", each with aria-pressed, instead of three different "label: value" constructions.
  • The header counts what matters: 8 slides, 1 hidden, 2 starred. The build hash stays, the generation timestamp goes.

Q1 Approve the direction, or push back on a specific part?

My assumption: both surfaces as drawn above. If wrong: tell me which of the eleven changes to drop and I will build the rest.

The pieces are independent. Dropping the resting opacity, or keeping the instruction paragraph, does not break anything else.

Q2 Should the bar rest at 62% and wake on hover, or stay at full strength?

My assumption: it rests. If wrong: it stays solid and the slide keeps competing with it.

The case for resting: it floats over your own content and you are usually reading, not clicking. The case against: a control you cannot see at a glance is a control you forget exists, and a reviewer who has never met this deck may not discover it. A middle setting is to rest only after ten seconds without pointer movement.

  • a) Rests at 62%, wakes on hover or focus. (my recommendation)
  • b) Always full strength.
  • c) Full strength, then rests after ten idle seconds.

Q3 The bar becomes a 12px rounded rectangle rather than a full pill. Keep the pill?

My assumption: 12px radius. If wrong: it stays 999px and reads as a lozenge.

A pill with two or three items reads as a pill. A pill with ten reads as a lozenge, and the round ends waste horizontal space at exactly the corner of the slide where a heading usually sits. This is taste, and the brand does not rule on it.

Q4 Should the slide position live in the bar?

My assumption: yes, 4 / 22 at the left of the bar. If wrong: the bar says nothing about which slide its state controls are acting on.

The runtime already draws a slide counter at the bottom centre. Putting it in the bar means the answer to "star which slide?" is beside the star. The cost is one more thing in the bar, and a counter appearing twice unless the bottom one is removed. I would remove the bottom one, but that is a change to the deck runtime rather than the review layer, so it is your call.
